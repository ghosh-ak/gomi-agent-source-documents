## General collection rules

- Put waste out by 8:30 a.m. on the designated collection day.
- Use the required Kitami City designated bag or disposal ticket.
- Incorrectly separated waste or waste placed without the required designated bag may be collected and opened for inspection.
- Collection may be suspended because of weather or road conditions. Check Kitami City's website or official waste-sorting app for current notices.
- Waste from businesses and waste removed by contractors may follow different rules and must not be treated automatically as household waste.

## Waste categories

### `burnable`

- Japanese: 燃やすごみ
- Bag: Kitami City designated orange bag (橙色)
- Fee: Paid
- Normal frequency: Twice per week
- Core rule: Burnable material with a maximum dimension of 30 cm or less, unless a specific item rule states otherwise.
- Common examples: Food waste, paper scraps, small wooden products, clothing and cloth cut to the required size, leather goods, rubber goods, and hygienic plastic items.
- Preparation:
  - Drain food waste thoroughly.
  - Cut clothing and large cloth to 1 m square or less and write `切っています` on the bag when required.
  - Remove detachable metal parts and dispose of them according to their own category.
  - Items that are sharp or otherwise hazardous require the specific safety rule listed in the dictionary.

### `unburnable`

- Japanese: 燃やさないごみ
- Bag: Kitami City designated young-grass green bag (若草色)
- Fee: Paid
- Normal frequency: Twice per month
- Core rule: Non-burnable household items and burnable items larger than 30 cm, generally up to 1 m and 10 kg, unless a specific item rule states otherwise.
- Common examples: Ceramics, glassware, metal products, hard plastic products, small household appliances, mats, umbrellas, and mixed-material products.
- Preparation:
  - Wrap broken glass, knives, scissors, and other sharp objects in paper.
  - Write `危険` on the designated bag for wrapped dangerous items.
  - Remove batteries and rechargeable batteries from electrical products and dispose of them as hazardous waste.
  - If an eligible item fits inside the designated bag but protrudes partly, attach an unburnable-waste disposal ticket as instructed.
  - Items longer than 1 m or heavier than 10 kg are generally large-sized waste.

### `recyclable`

- Japanese: 資源ごみ
- Bag or bundling: Transparent or translucent bag, or tied bundles for paper depending on material
- Fee: Free
- Normal frequency: Once per week
- Keep categories separate. Do not combine cans, bottles, PET bottles, and different paper types indiscriminately.
- Main groups:
  - Paper: Newspaper, magazines, cardboard, paper cartons, and eligible miscellaneous paper
  - Beverage and food cans
  - Beverage and food bottles
  - Eligible PET bottles bearing the PET mark
  - Used cooking oil
- Preparation:
  - Paper must be separated by type and tied with string.
  - Rinse and dry cans, bottles, and PET bottles.
  - Do not crush cans or PET bottles.
  - Remove PET-bottle caps and labels and place them with plastic containers and packaging.
  - Metal bottle caps are unburnable waste.
  - Cosmetic, medicine, and oil bottles are not recyclable bottles; they are unburnable waste.
  - Dirty or uncleanable recyclable items must follow the applicable exception in the item dictionary.

### `plastic_container_packaging`

- Japanese: プラスチック製容器包装
- Bag: Transparent or translucent bag
- Fee: Free
- Normal frequency: Once per week
- Eligibility rule: Plastic containers or packaging that held or wrapped a product and became unnecessary after the product was removed or used. Typically marked with the `プラ` symbol.
- Common examples: Product bottles, caps, lids, food trays, cups, packs, shopping bags, wrapping films, tubes, nets, polystyrene foam, and protective packaging.
- Preparation:
  - Empty, wash, and dry before disposal.
  - Do not place items into smaller bags inside the main collection bag.
  - Remove bottle caps and pumps when instructed.
  - Break eligible polystyrene foam into pieces of about 15 cm.
- Exceptions:
  - If food contamination cannot be removed, dispose of the item as burnable waste.
  - If non-food contamination cannot be removed, follow the dictionary because some items become unburnable waste.
  - Plastic products that are not containers or packaging are generally not in this category.
  - Plastic straws, spoons, forks, and food dividers are burnable waste.
  - Plastic cords and binding bands must be cut to 5 m or less and disposed of as unburnable waste.
  - Cleaning bags and other bags that did not contain a purchased product are unburnable waste.

### `diapers`

- Japanese: 紙おむつ類
- Bag: Transparent or translucent bag, maximum 45 L
- Fee: Free
- Normal frequency: Twice per week
- Maximum weight: 10 kg per bag
- Eligible items: Disposable diapers, cloth diapers, disposable pants, urine pads, and baby wipes.
- Preparation: Remove solid waste before disposal.
- Excluded items: Waterproof sheets, pet diapers, and toilet sheets. These are burnable waste.
- Alternative: Eligible diaper waste may still be placed in the paid orange burnable-waste bag.

### `hazardous`

- Japanese: 有害ごみ
- Bag: Transparent or translucent bag
- Fee: Free
- Collection timing: On applicable burnable or unburnable collection days
- Separation rule: Separate each hazardous-waste type. Do not mix hazardous items with ordinary waste.
- Common examples:
  - Batteries and rechargeable batteries
  - Mobile batteries and small devices with non-removable batteries when eligible
  - Spray cans and cassette-gas cylinders
  - Lighters
  - Fluorescent tubes and light bulbs
  - Mercury thermometers and other mercury-containing products
  - Empty containers that held specified volatile or flammable liquids
- Preparation:
  - Use up the contents of spray cans and gas cylinders.
  - Do not puncture spray cans or gas cylinders.
  - Place fluorescent tubes and bulbs in their original case or wrap them in newspaper to prevent breakage.
  - Remove batteries from appliances whenever possible.
- Important size rule: Batteries or battery-containing products larger than the stated municipal limits may be items Kitami City cannot process. Consult the item dictionary.

### `large_sized`

- Japanese: 粗大ごみ
- Fee: Paid
- Collection method: Advance application required; do not place at the neighborhood waste station.
- General threshold: Usually more than 1 m or more than 10 kg, plus items specifically listed as large-sized waste.
- Procedure:
  1. Contact the Large-sized Waste Reception Center.
  2. Provide address, name, telephone number, item type, and quantity.
  3. Confirm collection date, location, and required number of tickets.
  4. Purchase and attach Kitami City large-sized-waste disposal tickets.
  5. Place the item at the agreed location by 8:30 a.m. on collection day.
- Reception telephone: 0157-22-0053
- Reception hours: Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., excluding public holidays and December 29 through January 3.

### `not_accepted_by_city`

- Japanese: 市が処理できないごみ
- Collection method: Not accepted at neighborhood waste stations or through normal city collection.
- Common examples: Air conditioners, televisions, refrigerators, freezers, washing machines, clothes dryers, computers and monitors, vehicle tires, lead-acid batteries, gasoline, kerosene, waste oil, propane cylinders, pesticides containing toxic substances, paint, thinner, pianos, fire extinguishers, needles, and engine-powered equipment.
- Action: Contact the retailer, manufacturer, authorized recycling route, medical institution, or specialist identified in the item dictionary.

### `small_appliance_dropoff`

- Japanese: 小型家電（無料）
- Fee: Free
- Collection method: Designated drop-off locations, not the neighborhood waste station.
- General drop-box opening: Approximately 30 cm by 40 cm.
- Remove batteries and rechargeable batteries before drop-off.
- Personal computers and appliances covered by the Home Appliance Recycling Law are excluded.

### `textile_dropoff`

- Japanese: 繊維資源（無料）
- Fee: Free
- Collection method: Designated drop-off locations, not the neighborhood waste station.
- Eligible examples: Reusable clothing, towels, blankets without filling, and sheets.
- Items must be clean and dry.
- Excluded examples: Wet, dirty, or strongly odorous textiles; stuffed bedding and cushions; very small fabric pieces; carpets; toilet-seat covers; and entrance mats.

## Decision rules for the prototype

Apply these checks in order:

1. Identify the exact object and its function, not only its material.
2. Check the item dictionary for an exact or close item match.
3. Check visible or user-confirmed condition: clean, washable, contaminated, broken, sharp, containing liquid, or containing a battery.
4. Check material and recycling marks such as `PET` and `プラ` when visible.
5. Check size and weight thresholds: 15 cm, 30 cm, 1 m, and 10 kg where relevant.
6. Check whether the item was used by a household, a business, or removed by a contractor.
7. Apply the exact dictionary instruction before the general category rule.
8. If required information is not visible, ask one focused follow-up question.
9. If no official rule supports the answer, return `uncertain` and refer the user to Kitami City's Waste Management Division.
