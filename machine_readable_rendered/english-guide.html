Burnable waste

  • Use the Kitami City designated orange bag.
  • Collection occurs twice each week according to the local calendar.
  • Put the bag in the neighborhood waste station by 8:30 a.m. on the collection day.

Typical items

  • Kitchen waste, including vegetable scraps, food leftovers, shells, eggshells, coffee grounds, and tea waste
  • Paper scraps, photographs, postcards, tissues, and cigarette butts
  • Small paper boxes and paper cartons with aluminum interiors
  • Clothing and cloth pieces that meet the size requirements
  • Leather and rubber goods, including shoes, gloves, rubber boots, belts, bags, and rubber balls
  • Stuffed toys measuring 30 cm or less
  • Hygienic items such as toothbrushes, straws, kitchen or bathroom sponges, disposable warmers, refrigerants, pet waste, pet litter, and flower-arrangement sponge
  • Plastic packaging that cannot be cleaned, when the official item rule directs it to burnable waste

Preparation

  • Drain kitchen waste thoroughly.
  • Remove dirt where required.
  • Cut rubber hoses to less than 1 m.
  • Remove detachable belt buckles and place them with unburnable waste.
  • Clothing and large cloth must follow the cutting and bag-label instructions in the Japanese source.
  • Return injection needles to the medical institution. Do not put needles in household waste.

Diapers

  • Eligible diaper waste may be placed in a transparent or translucent bag.
  • Remove solid waste before disposal.
  • Exact eligibility and limits are defined in general_rules.md and the Japanese item dictionary.

Unburnable waste

  • Use the Kitami City designated young-grass green bag.
  • Collection occurs twice each month according to the local calendar.
  • Put the bag in the neighborhood waste station by 8:30 a.m. on the collection day.

Typical items

  • Glassware, ceramics, mirrors, plates, rice bowls, vases, and flowerpots
  • Medicine and cosmetic bottles
  • Metal products such as kettles, pans, portable gas stoves, scissors, knives, spoons, forks, and metal caps
  • Hard plastic products, plastic tableware, buckets, washbasins, watering cans, hangers, toys, CD and DVD discs or cases, toolboxes, and specialty storage bags
  • Small household appliances not excluded by recycling law
  • Items made of mixed burnable and non-burnable materials
  • Qualifying large cloth, wooden, plastic, or mixed-material products listed in the dictionary

Preparation and safety

  • Rinse medicine and cosmetic bottles.
  • Wrap broken glass, knives, scissors, and other sharp items in paper.
  • Write キケン or 危険 on the designated bag containing wrapped dangerous items.
  • Remove gas cylinders from portable gas stoves and dispose of the cylinders as hazardous waste.
  • Remove batteries and rechargeable batteries from appliances and dispose of them as hazardous waste.
  • Cut electric cords and similar long flexible items to the length specified in the item dictionary.

Unburnable-waste disposal ticket

  • A ticket may be used when qualifying unburnable waste partly protrudes from the designated green bag.
  • The item must normally be no longer than 1 m and no heavier than 10 kg.
  • Tie qualifying items securely and attach the ticket.
  • Items exceeding the limits are generally large-sized waste.

Recyclable waste

  • Collection occurs once each week according to the local calendar.
  • Use transparent or translucent bags for cans, bottles, PET bottles, and used cooking oil.
  • Keep each recyclable type separate.
  • Tie paper by type using string.

Paper

  • Newspaper
  • Magazines, books, textbooks, catalogs, brochures, comics, and telephone books
  • Flyers and wrapping paper
  • Cardboard
  • Eligible paper cartons
  • Paper bags and tissue boxes after removing non-paper parts

Cans

  • Eligible beverage and food cans include aluminum cans, steel cans, food cans, candy cans, and milk cans.
  • Rinse and dry.
  • Do not crush.

Bottles

  • Eligible beverage and food bottles are recyclable.
  • Rinse and dry.
  • Remove lids and sort the lids according to material.
  • Metal lids are unburnable waste.
  • Cosmetic, medicine, and oil bottles are not recyclable bottles; follow the Japanese dictionary.
  • Bottles that cannot be cleaned become unburnable waste when specified by the official rule.

PET bottles

  • Only eligible bottles with the PET mark belong in this category.
  • Rinse and dry.
  • Do not crush.
  • Remove caps and labels and place eligible plastic caps and labels with plastic containers and packaging.
  • Follow the Japanese dictionary for bottles that cannot be cleaned.

Used cooking oil

  • Filter out solids.
  • Put the oil into an appropriate plastic bottle.
  • Close the cap firmly; follow the sealing instructions in the official guide.

Plastic containers and packaging

  • Use a transparent or translucent bag.
  • Collection occurs once each week according to the local calendar.
  • This category is for eligible plastic containers and packaging, generally bearing the プラ mark.
  • It is not a category for every plastic product.

Typical items

  • Product bottles for cooking oil, beverages, shampoo, rinse, detergent, and bleach
  • Shopping bags and product bags
  • Tubes for seasonings, toothpaste, and similar products
  • Food cups, trays, packs, and plastic bento containers
  • Egg cases and tofu packs
  • Plastic caps, lids, and removable pumps
  • Packaging films
  • Nets used to package vegetables and fruit
  • Polystyrene foam and protective packaging

Preparation and exceptions

  • Empty, rinse, and dry eligible items.
  • Remove pumps, caps, and lids when required.
  • Metal caps are unburnable waste.
  • Remove metal clasps from nets and place them with unburnable waste.
  • Break eligible polystyrene foam into pieces of approximately 15 cm.
  • Plastic packaging that cannot be cleaned of food contamination becomes burnable waste.
  • Polystyrene foam that cannot be resized, or has specified non-food contamination, may become unburnable waste.
  • Vinyl ties and plastic binding bands must be cut to less than 5 m and placed with unburnable waste.
  • The exact item rule in waste_dictionary.json takes priority.

Hazardous waste

  • Use transparent or translucent bags.
  • Separate hazardous-waste types rather than combining them indiscriminately.
  • Collection is on the applicable burnable and unburnable waste days shown in the local schedule.

Typical items

  • Alkaline, manganese, lithium, button, and rechargeable batteries
  • Mobile batteries
  • Spray cans and cassette-gas cylinders
  • Lighters
  • Fluorescent tubes and light bulbs
  • Mercury thermometers
  • Empty containers that held specified thinner, benzine, alcohol, white gasoline, or oil-based varnish

Preparation and safety

  • Empty spray cans and gas cylinders completely.
  • Do not puncture spray cans or gas cylinders.
  • Remove plastic caps and sort them according to the plastic-container rule.
  • Put fluorescent tubes and bulbs in their original packaging or wrap them in newspaper to prevent breakage.
  • Return batteries to participating retailers when the official guide recommends that route.
  • Gasoline, thinner, and other remaining flammable liquids require retailer, fuel-station, or specialist handling. Do not place them in household waste.

Small-appliance drop-off

  • Kitami City provides free drop-off collection for eligible small household electrical items.
  • Neighborhood waste stations are not the drop-off location.
  • The typical collection-box opening is 30 cm by 40 cm.
  • Remove batteries and rechargeable batteries before using the drop-off service.
  • Larger eligible items may be taken to the Clean Life Center according to official instructions.
  • Air conditioners, televisions, refrigerators, freezers, washing machines, clothes dryers, and personal computers are excluded.

Textile drop-off

  • Kitami City provides drop-off collection for eligible reusable clothing and textiles.
  • Items must be clean and dry.
  • Wet, dirty, or heavily odorous clothing is not accepted.
  • Very small textiles, stuffed bedding, cushions, carpets, entrance mats, and similar excluded items must follow their dictionary rules.

Items Kitami City does not collect normally

  • Air conditioners
  • Televisions
  • Refrigerators and freezers
  • Washing machines and clothes dryers
  • Personal computers and computer monitors
  • Vehicle and motorcycle tires
  • Lead-acid batteries
  • Gasoline, kerosene, and waste oil
  • Propane cylinders
  • Paint and thinner
  • Pesticides containing toxic substances
  • Pianos
  • Fire extinguishers
  • Injection needles
  • Some engine-powered equipment
  • Use the retailer, manufacturer, authorized recycling channel, medical institution, or specialist identified in the Japanese item dictionary.

Large-sized waste

  • Large-sized waste requires advance application.
  • Do not leave it at a neighborhood waste station.
  • Large-sized Waste Reception Center: 0157-22-0053
  • Reception hours: 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday, excluding public holidays.

Procedure

  • Contact the reception center.
  • Provide the address, name, telephone number, item type, and quantity.
  • Confirm the collection date, disposal location, and required number of tickets.
  • Purchase the required Kitami City large-sized-waste ticket.
  • Attach the ticket and place the item at the agreed location by 8:30 a.m. on collection day.
  • Workers do not remove items from inside the home.

Self-delivery to the Clean Life Center

  • Households may deliver properly separated waste directly to the Clean Life Center.
  • The guide states a processing fee of 50 yen per 10 kg for the referenced edition.
  • Designated orange or green bags and disposal tickets are not required for self-delivery.
  • Prevent waste from scattering or falling during transport.
  • Verify current opening hours and fees before relying on this information operationally.
